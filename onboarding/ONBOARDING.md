# Onboarding: the creator's style (runs once)

The first time someone uses the kit, before their first video, build their style pack: every look decision for
their videos, in `HOME/style/` (HOME as SKILL.md finds it, `~/reel-machine` by default). It sits outside the
skill folder, so updating the kit never touches it.

- `style.json`: the values (fonts, colours, caption treatment, grade, pace, sound). Schema: `DEFAULT` in
  `pipeline/style.py`; what each colour means: `kit/TOKENS.md`.
- `STYLE.md`: the same style in words, for every agent that builds a card or an edit (template below).
- `brand.css` and `fonts/`: generated by `PY pipeline/pack.py build harbor` (the default pack; `build <name>` for
  another). Never edit by hand.
- `board/`: the style board they approve.

Everything in the pack comes from this creator: their brand, the videos they point to, their answers. The kit's
`DEFAULT` is a neutral fallback, not a look to steer toward. Ask in plain words, one step at a time.

## 1. Brand file
Ask: "Do you have a brand file? A brand guide, a DESIGN.md or brand.md, a PDF, or your website all work."
- **Yes**: read it. Take the fonts (headline, body, any mono), the colours (primary -> accent, backgrounds ->
  light and dark, text -> ink), corner radius, words for the feel, and any rules about what never to do. For a
  website, open it (a browser tool if you have one, else fetch the HTML and its CSS) and read the fonts and colours
  it actually uses.
- Then still offer step 2: a brand file sets colours and fonts but rarely says how videos should move. "Got it.
  Want to send 2 or 3 videos whose editing style you like? Optional, but it sharpens the pace and captions."
- **No**: go to step 2.

## 2. Inspiration: 2 or 3 videos
Ask: "Send me 2 or 3 links to videos whose style you want: Reels, TikToks, Shorts or YouTube. I'll study them."

Run `PY onboarding/refs.py <out dir> <link or file> <link or file> [...]` (out dir: `HOME/style/refs/`).
Per video it writes `metrics.json` (cuts and jump cuts per minute, shot length, words per minute, pauses, music
bed, loudness, motion, palette), a transcript, and contact sheets with times burned in: `sheet-<k>.jpg` (frames
across the video) and `captions-<k>.jpg` (the caption area up close), in pages small enough to read. One
`summary.md` compares them all and lists every page. Read the summary, then LOOK at every page it lists before
deciding anything: numbers say how fast, pictures say how it looks. (The full-height `sheet.jpg` and
`captions.jpg` exist too, but an image reader shrinks them past legible.)

Limits to keep in mind: Instagram usually wants a login, so ask for the saved file when a link fails; music is
judged by loudness alone (a loud room can read as a bed); sound effects are not measured; only the first 3
minutes of a video are analysed.

Read off:
- Mode: produced (cards, two framings, a sit-down setup: hi-fi) or selfie (phone in hand, overlays: lo-fi).
- Captions: box or plain, case, size, where they sit, the kind of face (serif, sans, rounded, condensed).
- Colour: the palette, the one colour that means "look here", light or dark leaning.
- Pace: cuts per minute and shot length (under 15 a minute calm, 15-30 normal, over 30 punchy).
- Motion: still frames, slow pushes, or hard zooms. Sound: music under the voice or not, how many effects.

Take the style, never the identity: no logos, handles, names or signature graphics of the creators they point to.
When they also gave a brand file, the brand wins on colours and fonts; the videos decide pace, captions and motion.

Links that fail (private, removed, blocked): ask them to save the videos and send the files; refs.py takes files.

## 3. Neither: four questions
Ask in one message:
1. Feel: calm and editorial, bold and punchy, warm and friendly, or clean and minimal?
2. One colour that feels like you? (Or "pick for me".)
3. Captions in a box or plain, normal case or ALL CAPS?
4. Do your videos lean light or dark?

Starting points by feel (Google Fonts; adjust to the colour and answers):

| feel | display | text | mono | grid | radius | motion | sfx |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| calm, editorial | Newsreader, Fraunces or Source Serif 4 | Inter or IBM Plex Sans | JetBrains Mono | on | 4 | calm | light |
| bold, punchy | Archivo Black, Anton or Bebas Neue | Archivo or Inter | IBM Plex Mono | off | 0 | punchy | dense |
| warm, friendly | Fraunces (italic) or DM Serif Display | DM Sans or Nunito Sans | DM Mono | on | 12 | normal | normal |
| clean, minimal | Inter Tight or Manrope | Inter | JetBrains Mono | off | 6 | calm | light |

How each mode edits, by feel (the `modes` section; `kit/STYLE_PACK.md`):

| feel | hi-fi hook | hi-fi camera | lo-fi captions | transitions (showcase) | emphatic word | j_lead |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| calm, editorial | two-line | settle | word-by-word, 4 words | push, reveal, zoom | stamp | 0.30 |
| bold, punchy | one-line | zooms | single, caps | whip, zoom, spin, drop | slam | 0.15 |
| warm, friendly | two-line | settle | phrase, 3 words | push, reveal, drop | scale | 0.30 |
| clean, minimal | one-line | still | word-by-word, 3 words | push, reveal | scale | 0.20 |

`examples/packs/` has three complete packs to start from or compare against: `default` (neutral), `bold` and
`soft`. Copy one to `HOME/style/` and change it, or write a new one.

## 4. Write the pack
- `style.json`, every field. Fonts must be on Google Fonts (style.py downloads them): for a paid brand font use the
  closest Google Font and tell them which. `fonts.caption` is the lo-fi caption face (often the text face, or the
  display face for a serif look).
- Colours: `accent` is for dark grounds and footage, `accent_on_light` a darker shade that reads on the light
  ground. Negative and positive keep their meaning (losses, gains); if the brand colour is red, keep it as accent
  and move negative to a clearly different hue.
- `captions`: box or plain, uppercase, size (56 hi-fi and 66 lo-fi are the defaults).
- `grade`: a CSS filter. Straight unless the inspiration has a clear look: warm
  `sepia(.08) saturate(1.08) contrast(1.04)`, cool `hue-rotate(-6deg) saturate(.92) contrast(1.05)`, contrasty
  `contrast(1.12) saturate(1.05)`, muted `saturate(.8) contrast(1.06)`.
- `motion`: calm, normal or punchy. `sound.sfx`: light, normal or dense. `sound.music`: "none" (they add music in
  their posting app; a connected music generator can change that per video).
- `creator`: name, handle, site. Ask once; used only on an end card, and only when they ask for one.
- `modes`: how hi-fi, lo-fi and showcase each edit for this creator (every field and how to read it off a brand
  file or a video: `kit/STYLE_PACK.md`). This is where their inspiration matters most: the caption rhythm, the
  camera language, the transitions and how a big word lands all come from what their references do, and they carry
  across all three modes so every video feels like the same person. A brand file rarely says how videos move:
  without inspiration, take the row for their feel above and confirm it on the board.
- `STYLE.md` from the template below, including a section per mode.
- Run `PY pipeline/pack.py build harbor`: it writes `brand.css` and `fonts/` (with the pack's `local-fonts/` and
  `extra.css`, which `style.py` alone would drop) and prints a WARNING for any colour pair that will read badly
  (low contrast, or emphasis and losses that look alike). Fix every warning before the board: keep the brand colour
  wherever it passes, and use a lighter or darker shade of the same hue only on the ground where it fails (that is
  what the `_on_light` fields are for). Write each such variant into STYLE.md so it is a choice,
  not a drift.

## 5. The board
`PY onboarding/board.py --clip <one of their videos, if they have one>` -> `style/board/index.html`: their type,
their colours and what each means, a frame of their own footage in each mode (the hi-fi hook and captions, the
lo-fi text box and captions as their `modes.lofi` says, the showcase emphatic word), three cards in their style.
Show it:
- With Claude artifacts: publish `board/index.html` with every file listed in `board/files.json` (icon "palette").
- Otherwise: run `python3 storyboard/serve.py <style dir>/board` in the background and send them the address.

Ask: "Does this look like you?" Apply what they say to `style.json` and `STYLE.md`, rerun `pack.py build harbor`
and `board.py`, show it again. When they say yes, the first video starts.

## Changing it later
"Update my style" runs steps 4 and 5 again on the current pack. Projects keep the copy they were made with
(`pipeline/new.py` copies the pack in), so old videos still render the same.

## STYLE.md template
```markdown
# <Name>'s style

<One paragraph in their words: the feel, who the videos are for, what they must never look like.>

## References
- <link>: <what to take from it: the caption box, the pace, the way numbers land>

## Type
- Display: <family>, <italic or upright>: hooks, thesis words, big figures
- Text: <family>: captions, labels, text boxes
- Mono: <family>: every number, unit, kicker

## Colour
- Accent <hex>: emphasis. Negative <hex>: losses. Positive <hex>: gains.
- Grounds: light <hex>, dark <hex>. <Grid or flat>, <radius>px corners.

## Captions
<box or plain, case, size; anything particular, like one emphasis word per line>

## Motion
<calm, normal or punchy, and what that means for them: entrances, zooms, how often something new lands>

## Sound
<effects density; music none unless they hand over a track>

## Hi-fi
<How a produced video edits for them: the hook (two lines, one big line, or none), how captions are cut, what goes
behind the head and how often, how often a card carries a beat, what the camera does on cuts, black and white or
not. Name the reference that shows it.>

## Lo-fi
<How a selfie video edits for them: caption rhythm (word by word, a phrase at once, one word at a time) and
position, text boxes or not, what lands on top of them and what swaps in behind them, zooms or not.>

## Showcase
<How a show-off video edits for them: how one big word lands, which transitions fit (and which never), how busy
it gets, and a motif idea if one suits them.>

## Never
- <their never-do list: from the brand file, the inspiration, or what they said>
```
